Fraud screening: a vendor we ask, and a vendor we feed

Fraud vendors (Sift, Riskified, Signifyd, Forter, Kount, Stripe Radar and others) are third parties. We never see their models. We send them a transaction, get back a signal, and later tell them how it turned out. This extension is the best example in the course of two integration directions with different timing: a synchronous inquiry in the middle of checkout, and an asynchronous feed of outcomes afterwards.

What we build, and what we don't

We build the Fraud Screening context and a fake vendor with scriptable behaviour. There is no real vendor integration. The goal is to model it so well that swapping the fake for a real one is one adapter. The fake can be a small in-process adapter first; if you want real HTTP and real timeouts, run it as a tiny separate service in Compose (fake-fraud-vendor) that listens for inquiries and posts decisions back.

What a screening integration actually looks like

Vendors differ in detail, but the shape is remarkably stable. This is the model we'll use (it's a sketch from common industry practice, not any one vendor's API):

Two kinds of bad news, kept apart

Card schemes and alert networks have their own names and codes: Visa's TC40 and Mastercard's SAFE fraud reports, and pre-dispute alert services such as Ethoca and Verifi. Don't let those names into the model, but don't merge the two kinds either. A fraud report is a signal: it teaches risk (ours and the vendor's) and changes nothing about the money. A pre-dispute alert is a request for action with a deadline. The model has one neutral type for each, IssuerFraudReport{Source, TransactionRef, ReportedAt, FraudType} and PreDisputeAlert{Source, TransactionRef, ReceivedAt, RespondBy}. Each feed you connect is one adapter translation into one of them.

Where it sits in checkout

                     └─ Allow  ─┐
  authorize payment (E5) ◄──────┘
  (optional) SCREEN again, stage BeforeCapture
  confirm seats
  capture
     │
     └─ events ─► Fraud context ─► TransactionOutcome feed to the vendor
The principle (ADR-E14)

The vendor gives us a signal; we make the decision. The vendor's verdict and score are inputs to a RiskPolicy in our domain, which also knows our own rules (allow-list for staff, force review above a value, block on repeat declines, what to do when the vendor is down). That keeps the business rule testable without the vendor and stops a vendor change from silently changing our risk appetite.

Rules

IdRule
FRD-1Each order is screened at most once per stage. Re-screening the same (orderRef, stage) returns the stored assessment.
FRD-2A RiskAssessment ends in exactly one Decision: Allow, Block, or Review (which later resolves to Allow or Block exactly once).
FRD-3Unavailability policy: if the vendor times out or errors, the decision is set by configuration: fail-open (Allow, flagged Unscreened) or fail-closed (Review, or Block above a configured value). Orders above a configured value never fail-open. The default is documented and tested.
FRD-4Our RiskPolicy may override the vendor: an allow-listed customer is never Blocked by a score alone; any order above the review threshold is at least Review.
FRD-5Every payment and order outcome for a screened transaction is reported to the vendor at least once, in per-transaction order, idempotently, even if the vendor is down for hours. Reporting never blocks or fails a customer-facing flow.
FRD-6Device and network signals are optional. A request without a fingerprint is valid and is screened with fewer signals; the absence is recorded, not an error.

E6.1 Model first: language, signals and the vendor firewall

Goal
Draw the model on paper and decide what is ours.
Rules
FRD-2, FRD-6
Your turn (paper first)
  1. Event-storm: order screened → Allow; → Block; → Review → approved later; vendor down; the order is later charged back. Where does each fact come from, us or the vendor?
  2. Fill in a vocabulary table in the style of E5's: vendor words (score, decision, approve/decline/review, device_id, reason_codes) against ours (Signal, Decision, DeviceSession, RiskReason). Which vendor terms deserve no counterpart in our domain?

E6.2 RiskAssessment and RiskPolicy

Goal
The domain: a small aggregate and one domain service, no vendor in sight.
Rules
FRD-1, FRD-2, FRD-3, FRD-4
Creates
internal/fraud/domain/{assessment,signal,policy,decision,events,errors}.go + tests
TDD script
  1. Open(orderRef, stage, now) → a pending assessment; Record(signal, policy, now) turns a vendor Signal{Level, Reasons} into a Decision via the policy, recording AssessmentDecided.
  2. RiskPolicy (a pure domain service, table-tested): Low → Allow; Medium → Review; High → Block; high-value order + Medium → Block-or-Review per config; allow-listed customer + High → Review (never auto-Block; FRD-4).
  3. Unavailable vendor: RecordUnavailable(now) applies FRD-3 for the given order value and config: fail-open → Allow with Unscreened; fail-closed → Review (or Block above the configured value).
  4. Review → ResolveReview(approved, by, now) exactly once; a second call is rejected or a no-op (decide, say why).
  5. Replaying a decided assessment with a different signal doesn't change its decision (FRD-1/FRD-2).
Discuss

Thresholds change weekly and are a business decision. Where do they live so that changing them needs neither a code change nor a vendor change? What would you test to be sure a config change can't silently open the gates?

E6.3 The FraudVendor port and a scriptable fake

Goal
Define the two directions as two ports, and build a fake that misbehaves on demand.
Creates
fraud/application/ports.go, adapters/driven/fakevendor
// Two driven ports, because the two flows fail differently.
type Screener interface { // sync: we wait, with a deadline
    Screen(ctx context.Context, r ScreeningRequest) (ScreeningResult, error)
}
type OutcomeReporter interface { // async: we retry; the vendor may be down
    Report(ctx context.Context, o TransactionOutcome) error // idempotent on o.ID
}
// Inbound, via a driving adapter: ReviewResolved, IssuerFraudReport,
// PreDisputeAlert.
TDD script (a contract suite, again)
  1. Scriptable by the request itself so tests are readable: an email like block@fraud.test → High; review@… → Medium; slow@… → sleeps past the deadline; boom@… → 500; otherwise Low.
  2. The fake honours the request's context deadline (a fake that ignores it hides bugs).
  3. The fake OutcomeReporter is idempotent on the outcome id and can be told to fail N times, then succeed; it records order of arrival so tests can assert ordering.
  4. The fake can later post a ReviewResolved, an IssuerFraudReport or a PreDisputeAlert into the webhook adapter.

E6.4 The ScreenOrder use case: timeouts and a circuit breaker

Goal
Keep a sluggish vendor from taking checkout down with it.
Rules
FRD-1, FRD-3
TDD script
  1. The use case wraps the call in context.WithTimeout from config. A slow vendor produces RecordUnavailable, not a hung request.
  2. Second call for the same (orderRef, stage) returns the stored decision without calling the vendor (FRD-1).
  3. A small circuit breaker (a decorator in platform/resilience: closed → open after N consecutive failures → half-open probe): when open, we skip the call and apply the unavailability policy immediately. Test it with a fake clock (ADR-008).
  4. Metrics (E3): fraud_screenings_total{decision,source=vendor|policy|unavailable} and a latency histogram. A rising unavailable share is the alarm.
Discuss

Fail-open sells tickets to fraudsters during an outage; fail-closed turns away every customer. Why is the right answer different for a $30 ticket and a $3,000 package, and what part of the system lets you express that?

E6.6 Asynchronous manual review

Goal
The vendor answers "Review" now and gives the real verdict minutes or hours later. Resume the saga when it does.
Rules
FRD-2
TDD script
  1. A ReviewResolved webhook (signature-verified like E5.8, translated in the adapter) calls ResolveReview and publishes fraud.assessment_decided.v1.
  2. Ticketing consumes it: Allow → continue to authorization; Block → Rejected and the hold is released. Duplicate and late deliveries are harmless.
  3. Race: the hold expires while the order is under review. The review later says Allow → the saga must not authorize payment for an expired hold (TKT-8 mindset). Write this test first; it's the interesting one.
  4. Staff with fraud:review can also resolve a review by hand through an HTTP endpoint (E2), audited (E4).

E6.7 Outcome feedback: reliably updating a service we don't control

Goal
Consume Payments' and Ticketing's events and tell the vendor what happened, without ever blocking a customer.
Rules
FRD-5
Creates
fraud.outcome_queue table, an outcome translator (event → TransactionOutcome), a worker, the real/fake OutcomeReporter
Same pattern, other direction (ADR-E15)

The transactional outbox solves "reliably tell our own Kafka". Calling a third party's API has the same failure modes (down, slow, duplicate) but a different sink, so we use a durable outbound work queue: a table written in the same transaction as the consumer's inbox row, and a worker that delivers with retry, backoff and a dead-letter state. Notice it is the outbox idea reused, not a new mechanism.

TDD script
  1. Translator (pure function, table-driven): payment_authorized → Authorized; payment_declined{InsufficientFunds} → Declined{...}; payment_captured → Captured; payment_voided → Voided; refund_succeeded → Refunded (with the amount, since refunds can be partial); dispute_opened → Chargeback; an IssuerFraudReport → FraudReported (vendors want these even when no chargeback follows). Unknown events are ignored explicitly.
  2. Consuming an event enqueues exactly one outcome (inbox de-duplication, 8.4).
  3. Worker with the fake reporter failing three times then succeeding: delivered once, attempts recorded, backoff grows (fake clock). After N attempts → DeadLettered, a metric increments, and an operator can requeue.
  4. Ordering: outcomes for one transaction are delivered in the order they happened (Authorized before Captured before Refunded). Use a per-transaction sequence number and have the worker deliver only the next one, or send the sequence so the vendor can order it. Test with a scrambled queue.
  5. The vendor being down for an hour does not slow a single checkout. Prove it with the fake's "down" mode and the e2e suite.
  6. Reports are idempotent on outcome.ID (derive it from the source event id) so a retry after a timeout cannot double-report.
Discuss

Why not call the vendor directly from the Payments context when a capture succeeds ("it's one HTTP call")? Trace what happens to the capture, to the customer, and to the vendor's model when that call fails.

E6.8 Chargebacks, fraud reports and pre-dispute alerts close the loop

Goal
Handle bad news that arrives from the outside, and teach the vendor from it.
Rules
FRD-5, plus PAY-8 and TKT-17 from E5
TDD script
  1. An IssuerFraudReport (a TC40-style feed, translated by the adapter) → fraud.fraud_reported.v1. Nothing happens to the money: Payments ignores it. It feeds the vendor (E6.7) and the PriorFraud signal below.
  2. A PreDisputeAlert webhook (translated by the adapter) → fraud.pre_dispute_alert.v1.
  3. A policy decision in Payments (not Fraud): on an alert for a Captured payment, proactively refund when the alert says it will otherwise become a dispute and the amount is under a threshold. This is cheaper than a chargeback fee. Fraud only tells; Payments decides what to do with its money. Test both sides separately.
  4. Ticketing must cope with a refund it didn't ask for: its refund_succeeded consumer (E5.9) moves a Paid or Fulfilled order to Refunded and voids its tickets even when the order wasn't RefundPending.
  5. The matching outcome (FraudReported / Chargeback) is queued for the vendor via E6.7, so it learns the transaction was bad. Check that the feed contains a fraud-coded chargeback and keeps the earlier Captured first.
  6. A charged-back customer never reaches screening: the denylist (E5.12) refuses them first. A fraud report doesn't block anyone, but it is a risk signal: add a PriorFraudReport flag to the screening request, fed by a small local projection built from fraud_reported events (a read model, as in 6.5).
  7. Policy decision to make and test: when the vendor's review or a staff member confirms fraud, Fraud asks the denylist to block the customer, email, card and device (a new device kind, DNY-1) with reason fraud_confirmed. Fraud decides; Denylist only stores and answers.
Discuss

The denylist and the fraud vendor both judge customers. When should a decision be a hard block in our own list, and when only a signal to the vendor? Which one would you trust with a customer who has spent thousands with you?
