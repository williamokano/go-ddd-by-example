Pricing: dynamic prices, discounts, fees and taxes

So far a seat costs exactly what the promoter wrote in the price list. Real ticketing prices move with demand, get discounted, carry fees and owe tax, and every cent of that has to add up, survive refunds, and be explainable to the customer. This extension is almost pure domain modelling: value objects, domain services, one aggregate learning a new trick, and very little infrastructure. It's the best place in the track to practise TDD on rich, rule-heavy code.

How this relates to lesson 9.8

Stretch 9.8 asks for a service fee and VAT in a PricingPolicy. This extension is the full treatment of the same area. If you did 9.8, you'll recognise E1.6–E1.7 and can reuse your code; E1.9 revisits 9.8's idea of fees as a domain service once the rules justify one.

Who owns which price?

"Price" means different things in different contexts. Pin it down before writing any code:

TermOwnerMeaning
List priceShow (SHW-4)What the promoter set per section. It arrives in Ticketing through the show.published.v1 snapshot and doesn't change in this extension.
Selling priceTicketingThe list price after demand adjustment, at the moment a customer holds a seat. Locked for that hold.
DiscountTicketingA reduction from a promotion: automatic (early bird, group) or a promo code.
FeeTicketingWhat Stagehand charges on top: a per-ticket service fee and a per-order fee.
TaxTicketing, using the venue's jurisdictionVAT or sales tax, either included in the price or added to it, depending on the country.
Price breakdownTicketingThe itemised, all-in result for an order: what the customer pays and why.
Design decision (ADR-E02): pricing is a module of Ticketing, not a new context

Every one of these numbers has to be consistent with a specific hold and order, which Ticketing owns. So pricing lives in internal/ticketing/domain/pricing: a sub-package of the domain, pure Go, imported by Ticketing's domain and application layers and by nobody else. If a revenue-management team later owns pricing rules, it could become its own context. The package boundary makes that move cheap.

The pricing pipeline

list price (Show snapshot)
   │  demand adjustment       DemandPolicy       E1.3  ┐ inside ShowInventory.Hold,
   ▼                                                    │ locked for the hold (E1.4)
selling price per seat ─────────────────────────────────┘
   │  discounts               DiscountPolicy     E1.5  ┐
   ▼                                                    │
discounted ticket price                                 │ at PlaceOrder,
   │  + fees                  FeeSchedule        E1.6  │ by the OrderPricing
   ▼                                                    │ domain service (E1.8)
   │  + taxes                 TaxPolicy          E1.7  │
   ▼                                                    │
PriceBreakdown (all-in total) ──► Order ──► payment ────┘

Rules

IdRule
PRC-1Money arithmetic never uses floating point. Amounts are integer minor units (Money), rates are integer basis points (1 bp = 0.01%), and rounding happens only at named steps, with one documented mode.
PRC-2The pipeline order is fixed: list price → demand adjustment → discounts → fees → taxes. Each step sees only the output of the previous one. Discounts never reduce fees or taxes.
PRC-3Demand pricing is off unless configured for the show. When on, a seat's selling price stays within a floor and ceiling of its list price (default 80%–150%), changes in steps, and is rounded to a price point (default: the nearest 0.50 of the currency).
PRC-4Price lock. The selling price is decided when seats are held and is what the customer pays for that hold, whatever demand does afterwards. All seats of one section in one hold get the same unit price. A preview quote is never binding.
PRC-5At most one promo code per order. Automatic discounts don't stack with each other (the best one wins), but the best one stacks with a promo code. Total discount is capped (default 50% of the discountable amount). An ineligible code is rejected with a reason, never silently ignored.
PRC-6The service fee is a rate of the discounted ticket price, clamped to a minimum and maximum per ticket. The order fee is a flat amount once per order. Fees are always shown and never discounted.
PRC-7The tax rule comes from the venue's country. A rule is either inclusive (the price already contains tax, as with EU VAT) or exclusive (tax is added, as with most US sales tax), and says whether fees are taxable. Tax is computed per line and summed.
PRC-8A breakdown is internally consistent: every amount ≥ 0, one currency, the total equals the sum of its lines, and the price shown to the customer is the all-in total.

E1.1 Money you can trust: rates, rounding and allocation

Goal
The arithmetic everything else is built on. Small, boring and completely reliable.
Rules
PRC-1
Concepts
Value object, shared kernel
Creates
internal/ticketing/domain/pricing/{rate,rounding,allocate}.go + tests
TDD script
  1. NewRate(bp int): 1000 is 10%. Negative rates are rejected. Is a rate over 100% valid? Yes for a demand multiplier (150%), no for a discount: make that a decision of the caller, not of Rate.
  2. ApplyRate(m Money, r Rate) Money rounds the result half up (away from zero): 10% of 0.05 = 0.01; 10% of 0.04 = 0.00. Table-test the boundaries.
  3. Allocate(total Money, weights []int64) []Money splits an amount by weights so the parts always sum to the total (largest-remainder method). Splitting 10.00 three ways gives 3.34, 3.33, 3.33, never 3.33 × 3 = 9.99.
  4. RoundTo(m, step): rounds to a price point such as 0.50 (needed by E1.3).
  5. A currency mismatch in any operation is an error, as Money already enforces.
Pitfall: the shared kernel

It's tempting to add Rate and Allocate to internal/sharedkernel. Don't, yet: ADR-009 keeps the kernel small because every change to it touches every context. They stay in Ticketing's pricing package until a second context really needs them. (Payments in E5 will need Allocate for partial refunds. That's when you have the conversation.)

Discuss

Half up or half even ("banker's rounding")? Half even removes a tiny upward bias over millions of operations; half up is what customers expect on a receipt. Pick one, record it as part of ADR-E03, and explain who could ever notice the difference.

E1.2 The PriceBreakdown value object

Goal
Model the result first. Every later step produces or refines one of these.
Rules
PRC-2, PRC-8
Creates
pricing/breakdown.go + tests
// A shape to react to, not to copy.
PriceBreakdown
  Lines []LineBreakdown          // one per seat
    SeatRef
    ListPrice      Money         // from the snapshot
    SellingPrice   Money         // after demand (locked at hold)
    Discounts      []Adjustment  // {Source: "early_bird" | "code:SPRING10", Amount}
    ServiceFee     Money
    Tax            TaxAmount     // {Rate, Amount, Inclusive bool}
  OrderFee         Money
  OrderFeeTax      TaxAmount
  Total            Money         // what the customer pays
TDD script
  1. A breakdown whose lines don't add up to Total can't be constructed (PRC-8). The constructor computes the total; nobody passes it in.
  2. Mixed currencies → error. Any negative component → error.
  3. Exclusive tax adds to the total; inclusive tax is reported but not added. Test both with the same numbers.
  4. Accessors the business will ask for: Subtotal(), TotalDiscount(), TotalFees(), TotalTax(), LineTotal(seat). The last one is what a per-ticket refund will need later.
  5. Equality by value: two breakdowns built from the same inputs are equal (cmp.Equal).
Discuss

Why store every component instead of just the total? Think about a support agent explaining a charge, a partial refund (E5.9), and a tax report. Which of these is impossible if only Total survives?

E1.3 Demand pricing as a pure policy

Goal
Turn "how full is this section, and how soon is the show?" into a price, with no aggregate and no clock in sight.
Rules
PRC-3
Concepts
Domain service as a pure function; ADR-008 (time is an input)
Creates
pricing/demand.go (DemandRules value object, DemandPolicy) + tests

Example rules, as data: occupancy ≥ 70% → +10%; occupancy ≥ 90% → +25%; less than 24 h before the show and occupancy < 50% → −15%. Floor 80%, ceiling 150%, price point 0.50.

TDD script
  1. DemandRules{} (the zero value) means off: the selling price equals the list price for every input. This is what keeps every existing test green in E1.4.
  2. policy.Price(list, occupancy, untilShow), table-tested: each tier, the boundaries (exactly 70%), and the last-minute markdown.
  3. Floor and ceiling clamp the result even with a silly configuration (+300%).
  4. The result is rounded to the price point with RoundTo from E1.1, and the rounding never crosses the floor or the ceiling.
  5. NewDemandRules rejects overlapping or unordered tiers and a floor above the ceiling.
  6. Occupancy is a value object (sold + held over capacity, per section). Building it from counts belongs to the aggregate (E1.4); the policy only reads it.
Discuss

Should held seats count toward occupancy, or only sold ones? A bot that holds 8 seats in a loop could push prices up for everyone. Which choice resists that, and what does it cost?

E1.4 The price lock: teach ShowInventory to quote

Goal
Decide the selling price at the moment of the hold, inside the aggregate that knows the occupancy, without breaking a single existing test.
Rules
PRC-3, PRC-4; extends TKT-2 and TKT-6
Touches (additively)
ticketing/domain/{inventory,hold,events}.go, the SeatsHeld event, the holds table (a price column per held seat)
Your turn (keep the suite green throughout)
  1. Give ShowInventory a DemandRules field, zero by default, and a new behaviour ConfigureDemandPricing(rules, now) that records DemandPricingConfigured. Refuse it on a closed inventory. Leave the signatures of OpenInventory and Hold alone.
  2. Inside Hold, compute each section's occupancy before the hold and ask the policy for the unit price. Store it on the Hold entity per seat, and add it to SeatsHeld.
  3. Run the whole suite. With zero rules every price equals the list price, so every existing test passes unchanged. If one doesn't, you've changed behaviour, not added it.
  4. Make PlaceOrder take its prices from the hold view instead of the section price list (TKT-6's "the sum of the line prices" now means the locked prices).
  5. Add the use case ConfigureDemandPricing and a Postgres column for the rules (JSON is fine; it's a value object). After E2 it gets a permission.
TDD script (new behaviour)
  1. With rules on and a section at 75%: a hold gets list + 10%, rounded to the price point.
  2. Price lock: a hold at 69% occupancy, then another customer's hold pushes the section past 70%: the first hold's price doesn't change, and its order uses the old price (PRC-4).
  3. Four seats in one section in one hold, even if the hold itself crosses a tier boundary, all get the same unit price: the one computed from occupancy before the hold.
  4. Released and expired holds give their seats back, so occupancy falls and the next quote can drop.
  5. Reconstituting an inventory and its holds from storage keeps the locked prices exactly.
Why inside the aggregate and not in the use case? (ADR-E04)

The occupancy and the hold must be consistent. If the use case read occupancy, computed a price and then called Hold, another hold could land in between and the price would be based on stale numbers. Passing the rules into the aggregate keeps the decision inside the consistency boundary that ADR-005 already pays for, and the policy itself stays a pure function you tested in E1.3.

Discuss

Dynamic pricing for live events is controversial with fans. Which rule in this lesson protects the customer, and which additional rule (published ceilings, a visible "price may change" notice, a per-customer cap) would you add if the business asked for fairness?

E1.5 Discounts: specifications and stacking

Goal
Model promotions whose eligibility rules change every month, without a growing if ladder.
Rules
PRC-2, PRC-5
Concepts
Specification pattern, policy
Creates
pricing/{promotion,specification,discount_policy}.go; a read-only Promotions port in application with an in-memory adapter
PromotionKindEligibility (specification)Benefit
Early birdautomaticordered more than 30 days before the show10% off tickets
Groupautomatic6 or more seats in the order5% off tickets
SPRING10codevalid between two dates, for these shows only10.00 off the order, allocated across tickets
FRONTROWcodesection ORCH only, at least 2 seats15% off the matching tickets
TDD script
  1. A Specification is IsSatisfiedBy(PricingContext) (bool, Reason), where PricingContext carries the order's lines, the show, the customer id and now. Write MinSeats, DaysBeforeShow, ValidBetween, ForShows, ForSections, plus And, Or, Not.
  2. An ineligible code returns why (ErrPromotionNotApplicable{Reason: "min_seats"}), which the HTTP adapter can show.
  3. Codes are normalised (trimmed, upper-cased) and unknown codes → ErrUnknownPromotion.
  4. Stacking (PRC-5): two automatic promotions → only the better one applies; one automatic plus a code → both apply; a second code → error.
  5. The cap: a 40% code on top of a 20% automatic discount is limited to 50% total.
  6. A fixed amount off the order is spread across the lines with Allocate (weighted by line price), so every line knows its share. A section-specific discount touches only its lines.
  7. Discounts reduce ticket prices only. Fees and taxes are computed afterwards from the discounted price (PRC-2), so a discount can never make a fee negative.
Discuss

"Max 100 redemptions" is a rule that crosses orders. Can a specification enforce it? (No: a specification sees one order.) Keep that thought for the stretch in E1.10.

E1.6 Fees

Goal
Add what Stagehand charges for its service, transparently.
Rules
PRC-6
Creates
pricing/fees.go (FeeSchedule value object) + tests; a FeeSchedules port with a config-backed adapter
TDD script
  1. Service fee 10% with a minimum of 1.50 and a maximum of 15.00 per ticket: a 10.00 ticket pays 1.50; a 40.00 ticket pays 4.00; a 300.00 ticket pays 15.00.
  2. The fee base is the discounted ticket price: a 20.00 ticket discounted to 15.00 pays 10% of 15.00 (subject to the minimum).
  3. The order fee (say 2.00) is charged once per order, not per ticket.
  4. A zero-priced ticket (a 100%-discounted comp, if the cap ever allows one) pays no service fee. Decide, test, document.
  5. The schedule is chosen per currency (an order fee in EUR can't be added to a USD order).
Pitfall: "drip pricing"

Showing a low ticket price and revealing fees at the last step is illegal for event tickets in several places (for example, the US FTC's 2025 rule on unfair or deceptive fees requires the total price, including mandatory fees, up front). That's why PRC-8 says the customer-facing price is the all-in total, and why fees live in the breakdown from the start instead of being bolted on at payment.

E1.7 Taxes, and the one fact no contract carries

Goal
Compute inclusive and exclusive taxes correctly, and get the venue's country into Ticketing.
Rules
PRC-7
Creates
pricing/tax.go (TaxRule, TaxPolicy) + tests; a TaxRules port with a config table
Touches (additively)
venue.activated.v1 and show.published.v1 each gain an optional country field; Show's VenueLayout projection (6.5) stores it
TDD script
  1. Exclusive: 8.25% on 39.99 → 3.299… → tax 3.30, line total 43.29.
  2. Inclusive: 20% VAT in a gross price of 48.00 → tax = 48.00 × 20 / 120 = 8.00; the line total stays 48.00. Write it in basis points: tax = round(gross × bp / (10000 + bp)).
  3. Fees taxable or not, per rule: with taxable fees, the service fee and the order fee get their own tax amounts.
  4. Per-line rounding: three lines of 0.10 at 25% exclusive → 0.03 + 0.03 + 0.03 = 0.09, not round(0.075) = 0.08. Document that tax is rounded per line (PRC-7), because the per-ticket refund will need it.
  5. An unknown country → ErrNoTaxRule, never a silent 0%.
  6. Some real rates don't fit in whole basis points (New York City's combined 8.875% is 887.5 bp). Decide whether Rate needs a finer unit, and change E1.1's tests first if it does.
Your turn: the missing fact
  1. Taxes depend on the venue's country, and today no event carries it. The snapshot principle (chapter 1: Ticketing never asks Venue or Show anything) tells you where it must go: into the snapshot.
  2. Add an optional country to venue.activated.v1; Show's projection stores it; show.published.v1 carries it; OpenInventory keeps it. Update the golden files and keep the "old payload still parses" test green: no v2 needed (that's 9.3's playbook for breaking changes).
  3. Inventories opened before this change have no country. Decide what checkout does for them (refuse, or use a configured default) and test it.
Discuss

This is the only change in the extension outside Ticketing's domain. Why couldn't Ticketing just look the venue up in the venue schema? Name the ADR it would break and the failure it would cause during an outage.

E1.8 Put it together: the OrderPricing service, checkout and a preview

Goal
One domain service that runs the pipeline; the order stores the result; the customer can see a quote before holding.
Rules
PRC-2, PRC-4, PRC-8; extends TKT-6
Touches
Order (stores a PriceBreakdown), the PlaceOrder/Checkout use case (one optional PromoCode field), the HTTP DTOs, the orders table
TDD script (outside-in)
  1. Acceptance (red): early bird on, demand on, 25% VAT inclusive, 10% service fee: a customer holds 2 seats, checks out with SPRING10, and the order total, the payment amount and the tickets-issued email all show the same all-in total, itemised.
  2. Domain: OrderPricing.Price(lockedLines, promotions, code, fees, taxRule, ctx) returns a PriceBreakdown, table-tested across combinations. This is a domain service: it coordinates value objects and policies and holds no state.
  3. Order.Place takes the breakdown; Total() is the breakdown's total; the payment port charges exactly that.
  4. Application: PlaceOrder loads the hold's locked lines, the active promotions, the fee schedule and the tax rule through their ports, calls OrderPricing, and passes the result to Order.Place. Still one aggregate saved.
  5. A query GET /shows/{id}/quote?seats=…&promo=… runs the same pipeline on the current demand price without holding anything, labelled non-binding (PRC-4).
  6. HTTP: the order response and the quote return the itemised breakdown as a DTO; promotion errors map to 422 with the reason.
  7. Old orders (placed before this extension) have no breakdown: reconstitute them with a one-line breakdown equal to their total, and test that path.
Discuss

Count the files you changed in this extension outside ticketing/domain. Now imagine the same features with pricing logic in HTTP handlers and SQL. Which tests would you be able to write, and how fast would they run?

E1.10 Stretch: redemption limits, a rule that crosses orders

Goal
"Each code can be used 100 times, once per customer." Decide the consistency you need and pay for exactly that.
Concepts
Aggregate boundaries, ADR-010 (sagas), ADR-011 (optimistic concurrency)
Your turn (paper first)
  1. Compare three designs: (a) a Promotion aggregate with a redemption count, reserved before placing the order and released if the order fails (a small saga); (b) count redemptions after the fact and accept a small overrun; (c) a unique constraint on (code, customer) plus a counter row updated with optimistic concurrency.
